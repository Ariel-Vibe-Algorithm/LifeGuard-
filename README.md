LifeGuard is a personal safety app. It gets help to you fast when you are in danger, even if you cannot make a phone call.

Status: working prototype. Nothing has been tested on a physical phone or watch yet. Do not rely on LifeGuard as your only way to get help. In an emergency, call 100, 101 or 102 (Israel).

Who it is for
People walking home alone at night, or riding with a driver they don't know.
Anyone meeting a stranger, for example on a first date.
Travellers abroad. The app shows the local emergency number.
Parents of children, and people at risk of falling.
Volunteers who want to help people nearby.
How it works
You start an alert by pressing SOS, using a keyboard shortcut, saying a secret code phrase, or letting a "walk me home" timer run out. The app then:

Sounds a siren, or stays silent if you used the silent phrase.
Records audio and photos as encrypted evidence.
Sends your live location to your trusted contacts, one at a time, until someone confirms.
Calls verified volunteers nearby if nobody answers.
Shows a walking route to the nearest police station, hospital or open public place.
Alerts go through the LifeGuard server, so you see who received the alert and who is coming. If your phone dies during a timed walk, the server raises the alarm.

Principles
Emergency first: nothing delays the SOS button.
Honest interface: the app says "safer according to the data", never "safe", and an old location is never shown as live.
Privacy: speech is recognised on the device, evidence is encrypted, and there is no remote listening.
No stigma: the map never labels a neighbourhood as dangerous.
Available for Windows, Android, Wear OS and iPhone (web app).
